Problem 1 | Mentor's frameworkOutpatient specialty clinic

Problem 1: Prior-authorization problems are documented; the preparation bottleneck remains unvalidated.

1Candidate cause and effect

Candidate cause: Changing payer rules and scattered records may make complete case preparation difficult. Effect: Missing evidence can create rework, consume staff time, and delay needed care; actual frequency and cause must be measured locally.

2Target user

A prior-authorization specialist in a medium-sized outpatient specialty clinic.

3Candidate bottleneck

Turning an unstructured payer policy into a complete, case-specific evidence packet before submission. Unvalidated: no national data yet measure incomplete clinic submissions.

Problem 2 | Mentor's frameworkMultispecialty medical group

Problem 2: Misrouted denials delay payment and leave patients uncertain about what they owe.

1Candidate cause and effect

Candidate cause: Denial information is often unclear and split across several systems. Effect: Misclassification creates queues and rework, delays correction or appeal, holds up provider payment, and can leave patients with unresolved bills.

2Target user

A denial-management specialist in a multispecialty medical group.

3Candidate bottleneck

Classifying each denial and routing it to billing, coding, clinical review, appeal, or write-off review. Feasibility depends on having case detail beyond broad public categories.

4Evidence

85 millionin-network claims denied in 2024; fewer than 1% appealed; 36% of reported reasons were “Other.”READ MORE ↗

5Success metric

First-pass routing accuracy confirmed by an authorized reviewer.

8 / 15 | KFF analysis of CMS data: about 496 million claims reported; 19% of in-network claims denied; fewer than 1% appealed; 66% of appealed denials upheld; 36% of reported denial reasons were “Other.” HealthCare.gov Marketplace plans only.

Problem 3 | Mentor's frameworkHospital to home

Problem 3: An incomplete discharge handoff can leave critical follow-up undone after the patient gets home.

1Candidate cause and effect

Candidate cause: Discharge follow-up depends on several teams, clear instructions, capacity, and confirmed ownership. Effect: When follow-up is not completed, needed care may be delayed; the specific causes must be measured locally.

2Target user

A discharge planner or nurse case manager sending adult medical patients home.

3Candidate bottleneck

Confirming that the receiving organization accepted responsibility for the next operational step.

4Evidence

38.8%received primary-care follow-up within 14 days in 2019; national analysis of 79.1 million eligible Medicare discharges.READ MORE ↗

5Success metric

Closed-loop handoff completion rate within an approved follow-up window.

10 / 15 | Anderson et al., JAMA Health Forum, 2024: 79,125,965 eligible Medicare discharges from 2010–2019; timely primary-care follow-up rose from 31.5% to 38.8%. This measures completed visits, not handoff quality or cause.

Potential agentic AI workflow (illustrative)

1Plan role: turns the approved discharge plan into assigned operational tasks.
2Handoff role: sends authorized information and confirms receipt and ownership.

Business model hypothesisSell an operational outcome, not another app listing

A focused B2B workflow pilot is our most defensible starting hypothesis.

Customer and buyer

Healthcare employer organization

A hospital, clinic, or medical group buys through the department accountable for the workflow, budget, and measurable result.

Initial offer

Paid, narrow workflow pilot

Configure one bounded use case, connect only approved data, retain human approval, and measure one agreed operational metric.

Revenue model

Implementation plus recurring subscription

Charge for setup and integration, then an organizational, site, user, or case-volume subscription. Pricing still requires buyer research.

Growth path

Prove, renew, then expand

Begin with one department and workflow. Expand to additional sites or related workflows only after verified savings, quality, and adoption.

Why not an app marketplace first? These concepts depend on sensitive data, workflow integration, governance, accountability, and enterprise purchasing. A marketplace may become a distribution channel later, but it is not the business model by itself.
Healthcare data and compliance boundary: Concept prototypes use public or synthetic data only. Any real deployment involving protected health information would require HIPAA Privacy and Security Rule review, minimum-necessary access, role-based permissions, audit trails, appropriate safeguards, approved integrations, and a Business Associate Agreement where applicable. Final requirements need qualified privacy, security, legal, and compliance review.
